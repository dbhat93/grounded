# Grounded: the feature-vs-company validation kit

Internal working doc. The point is not to validate the idea. It is to **kill it in an afternoon** if it is a feature, before anyone quits anything. Weight the NOs. If you finish wanting to believe, you ran it wrong.

## The one thing being tested

> In regulated FinServ sales, a rep giving a wrong or unvetted answer on a live call is a **material risk** (compliance, legal, reputational: consequences beyond the deal), frequent and painful enough that a risk-owning buyer would pay to prevent it, and it is **not already solved**.

If that sentence is true, Grounded is a company and the skeptic is wrong. If it is false, it is a feature and he is right. Everything below is designed to find out which, fast.

## Who to talk to

Not the bank. **The vendor selling into the bank**, because they eat the risk when their rep misspeaks. Target: VP Sales / Head of Sales Engineering / Head of Enablement / CRO / founder-CEO at companies that sell software into banks and credit unions. A skeptical founder-CEO who sells into regulated buyers is the archetype.

- **Sample:** 5 to 8 of them. One afternoon of calls.
- **Timebox:** 20 minutes each. You listen 80% of the time.

## Two rules you will want to break

1. **Never pitch, never say "would you buy."** The moment they know your idea, the data is poisoned and every answer becomes politeness. You are a researcher for 20 minutes, not a founder.
2. **Dig into the last real instance, not the hypothetical.** "Tell me about the last time" beats "do you ever" every time. Stated preference is worthless; you want stories, money, and past behavior.

Keep it clean-room: this is research about their world, not a demo of a product, and not built on any employer's data. The research framing handles that for you.

## The five questions

Each one can end the investigation. Ask them roughly in order. The follow-up probe matters more than the question.

### 1. Frequency: does it even happen?
> "Tell me about the last time one of your reps or SEs told a regulated prospect something about the product that was wrong, or called a roadmap feature GA. What happened?"

- **Probe:** How recent? Which deal? Who caught it?
- **Kills it if:** they cannot recall a single instance, or have to reach back years.
- **Alive if:** a story lands instantly, recent, with a deal or a name attached.

### 2. Consequence: does it follow them past the moment? (the thesis rides here)
> "When that happened, did it stay in the room, or did it follow you: into the deal, their security review, legal, the relationship?"

- **Probe:** What did it cost in time, dollars, or trust? Did anyone escalate it?
- **Kills it if:** "we sent a correction email, no big deal." A recoverable consequence means "beyond the moment" is a slogan, and the risk-buy is dead.
- **Alive if:** it blew up a security review, pulled in legal, cost the deal, or poisoned trust for months.

### 3. Ownership and spend: is there a buyer and a budget?
> "Whose job is it today to keep reps from saying the wrong thing in a regulated deal? What have you actually built for it: deal desk, approved answers, SE gating, battle cards? What does it cost in time or heads?"

- **Probe:** Is it a bottleneck? How many hours a week? Who complains about it?
- **Kills it if:** "nobody really, reps are expected to know." No owner, no budget, no sale.
- **Alive if:** a named owner and a real apparatus (a deal-desk bottleneck, an SE who reviews everything, headcount). Existing spend is a budget you can redirect.

### 4. The already-solved test: is the gap real? (the sharpest kill)
> "When accuracy is non-negotiable, an RFP or a security questionnaire, how does that get answered today? And how often does the live-call version get it wrong versus the written version?"

- **Probe:** What is the difference between what a rep can say live and what they would put in writing?
- **Kills it if:** "live is fine, reps know the answers, it is handled." No gap between airtight-written and wild-west-live means nothing to sell.
- **Alive if:** "the written stuff is locked down and reviewed, but live calls are the wild west." That gap is the entire product.

### 5. Revealed intent: have they already paid to fix it? (never "would you buy")
> "Have you ever held a rep back from a call, added a manual review step, or walked away from a deal because you were worried what they would say? Have you looked at Gong, Winn, an enablement tool for this? What happened?"

- **Probe:** What did you try, why did it stick or not?
- **Kills it if:** never thought about spending on it, never looked. Not top-of-mind is not a painkiller.
- **Alive if:** they have already spent money, time, or deals on it. Revealed preference beats every enthusiastic "I would totally buy that."

## Scoring

Per conversation, count how many come back **hot** (recent painful story / real lasting consequence / named owner plus spend / live-is-the-gap / already-paying).

| Hot (of 5), across most of 5 to 8 people | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 4 to 5 | **Validated.** The risk-buy is real. the skeptic is wrong. Go. |
| 2 to 3 | **Real but soft.** Probably a feature a budget-holder tolerates, not funds. Yellow. Dig into whether the consequence (Q2) is ever catastrophic, not just annoying. |
| 0 to 1 | **Kill it, or pivot.** On the sales side it is a feature. Re-run Q1 to Q2 for the FI member-interaction version (support/service calls); if those come back hot, switch companies before you switch jobs. |

## The trap

You will hear one great war story and fall in love. One story is an anecdote. The pattern across eight is the signal. **Spend more time on the shrug than the nod:** the person who says "eh, we sent an email" is more informative than the one who validates you. Your job is to find the strongest reason this is a feature, and only then decide it is not.

## What a pass unlocks / what a kill saves

- **Pass:** you have a fundable wedge and a buyer with a budget line. The product we built is proof you can deliver on the promise; the interviews are proof someone will pay for it. You need both.
- **Kill:** you learned in an afternoon what most founders learn in two years and a resignation letter. That is a win, not a loss.
