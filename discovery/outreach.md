# Outreach: booking the 20 minutes

What you actually send. The goal is a short call, not a reply thread, and definitely not a pitch. Frame everything as **research about their world**, never as "I built a thing, want to see it?" The moment they smell a product, the data is worthless and so is the call.

Do not send them the five questions. Those are yours. You send a request for time.

---

## Warm (primary): someone you know, or a light intro

**Short version (LinkedIn DM / text):**

> Hey [name], quick one and slightly random. I'm digging into how teams that sell into banks and credit unions keep reps from misstating things on a live call: security posture, what's GA vs roadmap, that kind of thing. You've lived this at [company]. Could I steal 20 minutes to hear how you actually handle it? Not selling anything, genuinely just learning. This week or next?

**Slightly longer version (email):**

> Subject: 20 min on a regulated-sales thing you've lived
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm spending some time understanding a specific problem in regulated sales: what happens when a rep or SE says something on a live call that turns out to be wrong or not-yet-shipped, and how teams selling into FIs deal with that. You'd have real scars here from [company].
>
> Would you give me 20 minutes to walk me through how it actually works on your side? I'm in learning mode, not selling anything. Happy to work around your calendar.
>
> Thanks either way,
> Dhiraj

---

## Cold (secondary): no relationship, need credibility fast

> Hi [name], I'll be quick. I'm researching how companies that sell into regulated FIs prevent reps from misstating compliance or product facts on live calls, and you run [SE / sales / enablement] at [company], which is exactly the seat I'm trying to understand. No pitch, no product: 20 minutes of you telling me how it really works would be genuinely useful. Worth a short call in the next week or two?

---

## Async fallback: only if they will not take a call

Weaker signal (you lose the follow-up dig, and written answers drift toward the polite hypothetical), but better than nothing. Send at most two questions, both about the past:

> Totally understand if a call's tough. If you're up for two lines by reply, even that helps a lot:
>
> 1. Last time a rep or SE said something wrong or not-yet-shipped on a regulated deal, what actually happened afterward?
> 2. Whose job is it today to keep that from happening, and what have you put in place for it?

Treat any async answer as half-weight versus a live conversation, and try to convert it into a call.

---

## Rules for the outreach itself

- **Research, not product.** No "I'm building," no "would you use," no link to a demo. You are learning how their world works.
- **Reference their scar tissue.** "You've lived this at [company]" earns the reply; a generic ask does not.
- **Ask for a small, specific thing.** 20 minutes, this week or next. Not "some time to chat."
- **Make the no easy.** "Not selling anything" and "happy either way" get more yeses than pressure does.
- **Keep it clean-room.** Nothing about a current employer's data, roadmap, or internals. The problem is industry-wide; talk about the industry.
